# Table 2: Definition of Misinformation vs. Regular Framing

The study classified framing based on the intersection of **Treatment Type** and **User Valence**. "Misinformation Framing" occurred when the user's stance contradicted medical consensus.

| Treatment Type | Misinformation Framing | Regular Framing |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Alternative Treatment**<br>*(e.g., Colloidal Silver)* | **Positive (Promoting)**<br>"Colloidal silver is a miracle cure for cancer."<br>*(Promoting unproven)* | **Negative (Questioning)**<br>"Is it true that colloidal silver is dangerous?"<br>*(Questioning unproven)* |
| **Traditional Treatment**<br>*(e.g., Chemotherapy)* | **Negative (Attacking)**<br>"Chemotherapy is just poison that kills you."<br>*(Attacking proven)* | **Positive (Neutral/Inquiry)**<br>"How does chemotherapy help with cancer?"<br>*(Asking about proven)* |

**Key Insight:** Both "promoting the bad" and "attacking the good" constitute misinformation framing in this study.
